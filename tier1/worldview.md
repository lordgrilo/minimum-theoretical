# Foundational Papers

These aren't textbooks. They're the papers behind the lab's approach: the
intellectual commitments that set it apart from a generic complex-systems group.
Read all of them; they're short. Five commitments: emergence is real and irreducible
(Anderson); information is physical (Shannon, Jaynes); beyond pairwise (Battiston);
the brain is a network but more than that (Petri 2014); cognition has mathematical
limits (Petri 2021, Shepard).

## Reading

- **Anderson (1972)** — *More is Different.* Science. Four pages that destroyed naive reductionism. New laws emerge at each level; why we study networks, not neurons, and why higher-order interactions carry irreducible information.
- **Shannon (1948)** — *A Mathematical Theory of Communication.* Bell System Technical Journal. The origin of information-theory. Information has quantifiable structure and obeys laws.
- **Jaynes (1957)** — *Information theory and statistical mechanics.* Physical Review. The bridge: statistical mechanics *is* inference; thermodynamic entropy *is* information entropy.
- **Shepard (1987)** — *Toward a universal law of generalization for psychological science.* Science. Exponential decay of generalization across species and modalities. The seed of the semanticity framework.
- **Miller (1956)** — *The magical number seven, plus or minus two.* Psychological Review. Working-memory capacity limits — later reinterpreted via semantic horizons as a 1/n collapse.
- **Smolensky (1990)** — *Tensor product variable binding and the representation of symbolic structures in connectionist systems.* Artificial Intelligence. The bridge between symbolic AI and connectionism; the representational mechanism behind the generalization–identification tradeoff.
- **Petri, Musslick, Dey, Özcimder, Turner, Ahmed, Willke & Cohen (2021)** — *Topological limits to the parallel processing capability of network architectures.* Nature Physics. Shared representations impose hard topological limits on multitasking. Structure constrains computation.
