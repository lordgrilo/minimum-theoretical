# Computational Practice & Epistemic Hygiene

Reproducible, honest computation. The lab makes ambitious "universal law" claims;
this is the minimum not to fool ourselves — data leakage, ablations, effect sizes
over significance, preregistration, negative controls — plus the shared stack.

## Reading

- ⭐ **Kapoor & Narayanan (2023)** — *Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science.* Patterns. Data leakage — the dominant failure mode in ML-for-science. [doi:10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804)
- **Wilson, Bryan, Cranston, Kitzes, Nederbragt & Teal (2017)** — *Good enough practices in scientific computing.* PLoS Computational Biology. A reproducible-workflow baseline. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005510](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005510)
- ⭐ **Gelman & Loken (2013)** — *The garden of forking paths: why multiple comparisons can be a problem, even when there is no “fishing expedition” or “p-hacking” and the research hypothesis was posited ahead of time.* Unpublished manuscript (related article: *The statistical crisis in science*, American Scientist, 2014). Researcher degrees of freedom and the multiple-comparisons trap in exploratory work. [link](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/unpublished/forking.pdf)
- **Nosek, Ebersole, DeHaven & Mellor (2018)** — *The preregistration revolution.* PNAS. When and why to preregister. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1708274114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)
- **The lab's computational stack** — see the Tools page. Python + NumPy/SciPy, NetworkX/XGI/HyperNetX, GUDHI/Ripser/giotto-tda, dit/POT, nilearn/MNE, PyTorch. See tools/computational_tools.md.
