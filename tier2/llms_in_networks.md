# Collective AI, Multi-Agent LLMs & Synthetic Social Systems

The across-agent counterpart of the VLM work. The defining question: when
synthetic agents interact, which collective phenomena carry over from human and
social systems, and which are artifacts of training, prompting or shared model
priors? Treats multi-agent LLM systems as social systems to be probed with the
lab's network and information-theoretic tools.

## Reading

- **Galton (1907)** — *Vox populi.* Nature. The historical anchor for the wisdom of crowds — aggregated estimates beat individuals.
- **Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing (2011)** — *How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect.* PNAS 108, 9020–9025. Social influence erodes the diversity that crowd wisdom needs: the independence assumption that ensembles of LLM agents break.
- **Du, Li, Torralba, Tenenbaum & Mordatch (2023)** — *Improving factuality and reasoning in language models through multiagent debate.* arXiv:2305.14325 (ICML 2024). Multiple LLM agents debate to converge on better answers — collective reasoning across synthetic agents.
- **Park, O'Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang & Bernstein (2023)** — *Generative agents: interactive simulacra of human behavior.* UIST '23. arXiv:2304.03442. Societies of LLM agents reproduce emergent social behaviour — an across-agent testbed.
- **Schrimpf, Blank, Tuckute, Kauf, Hosseini, Kanwisher, Tenenbaum & Fedorenko (2021)** — *The neural architecture of language: integrative modeling converges on predictive processing.* PNAS 118(45):e2105646118. LLM representations predict human language-network activity — the brain–LLM alignment anchor (seeds our brain–LLM alignment work).
- **Goldstein, Zada, Buchnik, …, Hasson (2022)** — *Shared computational principles for language processing in humans and deep language models.* Nature Neuroscience 25(3):369–380. Next-word prediction and contextual embeddings shared by brains and LLMs.
