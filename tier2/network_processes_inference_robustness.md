# Network Processes, Inference & Robustness

Dynamical processes on networks and the inferential turn: epidemics and
percolation, robustness and cascading failure, and the shift from describing
network structure to inferring it. Working depth for across-agent projects —
not part of the Tier 1 minimum.

## Reading

- **Albert & Barabási (2002)** — *Statistical mechanics of complex networks.* Reviews of Modern Physics. Framed network science as a branch of statistical physics.
- **Fortunato (2010)** — *Community detection in graphs.* Physics Reports. Comprehensive review of community detection.
- ⭐ **Barrat, Barthélemy & Vespignani (2008)** — *Dynamical Processes on Complex Networks.* Cambridge. Essential companion to Newman. First pass: Ch. 4–7 and 9 (phase transitions, resilience and robustness, synchronization, epidemic spreading).
- ⭐ **Pastor-Satorras & Vespignani (2001)** — *Epidemic spreading in scale-free networks.* PRL. Absence of an epidemic threshold in scale-free networks.
- **Pastor-Satorras, Castellano, Van Mieghem & Vespignani (2015)** — *Epidemic processes in complex networks.* Reviews of Modern Physics. Definitive review of two decades of epidemic spreading.
- ⭐ **Newman (2002)** — *Spread of epidemic disease on networks.* Physical Review E. Maps SIR epidemics onto bond percolation.
- ⭐ **Molloy & Reed (1995)** — *A critical point for random graphs with a given degree sequence.* Random Structures & Algorithms. The Molloy–Reed criterion for the giant component.
- ⭐ **Callaway, Newman, Strogatz & Watts (2000)** — *Network robustness and fragility: percolation on random graphs.* PRL. Robustness as percolation.
- ⭐ **Albert, Jeong & Barabási (2000)** — *Error and attack tolerance of complex networks.* Nature. Landmark robustness paper.
- ⭐ **Buldyrev, Parshani, Paul, Stanley & Havlin (2010)** — *Catastrophic cascade of failures in interdependent networks.* Nature. Landmark interdependent-networks paper.
- **Peel, Peixoto & De Domenico (2022)** — *Statistical inference links data and theory in network science.* Nature Communications. The shift from descriptive to inferential network science.
