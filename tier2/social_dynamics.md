# Social Dynamics & Collective Behavior

Information processing across populations of social agents: contagion, opinion
formation, language games, cooperation — and how higher-order structure changes
all of them.

## Reading

- ⭐ **Centola & Macy (2007)** — *Complex contagions and the weakness of long ties.* American Journal of Sociology. Simple vs complex contagion. [doi:10.1086/521848](https://doi.org/10.1086/521848)
- **Granovetter (1973)** — *The strength of weak ties.* American Journal of Sociology. Why network structure matters for social dynamics. [doi:10.1086/225469](https://doi.org/10.1086/225469)
- **Alvarez-Rodriguez, Battiston, de Arruda, Moreno, Perc & Latora (2021)** — *Evolutionary dynamics of higher-order interactions in social networks.* Nature Human Behaviour. Higher-order game theory. [doi:10.1038/s41562-020-01024-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-01024-1)
- **Daley & Kendall (1965)** — *Stochastic rumours.* IMA Journal of Applied Mathematics. The Daley–Kendall rumour model. [doi:10.1093/imamat/1.1.42](https://doi.org/10.1093/imamat/1.1.42)
- **Castellano, Fortunato & Loreto (2009)** — *Statistical physics of social dynamics.* Reviews of Modern Physics 81, 591–646. The review of opinion, cultural and language dynamics: voter model, Axelrod, naming game. [doi:10.1103/revmodphys.81.591](https://doi.org/10.1103/revmodphys.81.591)
- **Baronchelli, Felici, Loreto, Caglioti & Steels (2006)** — *Sharp transition towards shared vocabularies in multi-agent systems.* J. Stat. Mech. The naming game. [doi:10.1088/1742-5468/2006/06/p06014](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/2006/06/p06014)
- **Nowak & May (1992)** — *Evolutionary games and spatial chaos.* Nature. Mandatory for evolutionary game theory on networks. [doi:10.1038/359826a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/359826a0)
- **Santos & Pacheco (2005)** — *Scale-free networks provide a unifying framework for the emergence of cooperation.* PRL. Topology and cooperation. [doi:10.1103/physrevlett.95.098104](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.95.098104)
- **Szabó & Fáth (2007)** — *Evolutionary games on graphs.* Physics Reports. Comprehensive evolutionary-game review. [doi:10.1016/j.physrep.2007.04.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2007.04.004)
- **Shalizi & Thomas (2011)** — *Homophily and contagion are generically confounded in observational social network studies.* Sociological Methods & Research 40, 211–239. In observational network data, homophily and contagion cannot in general be told apart. [doi:10.1177/0049124111404820](https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124111404820)
- **Centola (2010)** — *The spread of behavior in an online social network experiment.* Science 329, 1194–1197. Behaviour spreads further in clustered networks, as complex contagion predicts. [doi:10.1126/science.1185231](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1185231)
- **Cencetti, Contreras, Mancastroppa & Barrat (2023)** — *Distinguishing simple and complex contagion processes on networks.* Physical Review Letters 130, 247401. Tells simple from complex contagion from the order in which nodes are infected. [doi:10.1103/physrevlett.130.247401](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.130.247401)
- **St-Onge, Iacopini, Latora, Barrat, Petri, Allard & Hébert-Dufresne (2022)** — *Influential groups for seeding and sustaining nonlinear contagion in heterogeneous hypergraphs.* Communications Physics 5, 25. Which groups seed and sustain nonlinear contagion on heterogeneous hypergraphs. A lab paper. [doi:10.1038/s42005-021-00788-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-021-00788-w)
