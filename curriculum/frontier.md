# Tier 3 · The Frontier

What the lab is building rather than using. Nothing here is required; it is where our open questions live.

## Semantic Horizons & Universal Laws

NPLab's most distinctive theoretical contribution and the flagship of the within-agent arc. A system encoding stimuli with finite precision Δ and compressing by β faces a *semantic horizon* r*(β) = −β log Δ beyond which stimuli become indistinguishable — producing non-monotonic generalization and a 1/n capacity collapse that recovers Miller's Law from first principles. The long-term bet (Keck) is that these are *universal* laws, the bridge from within-agent limits to across-agent systems (immune repertoires, epidemics, whale codas). The papers themselves are listed once, where the curriculum uses them: Bound by Semanticity and Savietto et al. (2026) under Semanticity & Cognition, and Petri et al. (2021) under Worldview (Tier 1).

- Cover & Thomas Ch. 10 (rate-distortion) + Tishby et al. (2000) IB.
  The mathematical engine: R(D) and the β-parameterized compression–relevance tradeoff with finite precision.

## Irreducible Multivariate Information

How to quantify genuine multivariate dependency — synergy, redundancy, integration — that pairwise measures cannot see, and how to estimate it without fooling yourself. This is the engine under our work on higher-order information in neural, clinical and developmental data.

- Rosas, Mediano, Gastpar & Jensen (2019), *Quantifying high-order interdependencies via multivariate extensions of the mutual information.* Physical Review E.
  Introduced the O-information (signed synergy/redundancy).
- Williams & Beer (2010), *Nonnegative decomposition of multivariate information.* arXiv:1004.2515.
  Foundational Partial Information Decomposition (PID).
- Timme & Lapish (2018), *A tutorial for information theory in neuroscience.* eNeuro.
  Practical guide to applying IT measures to neural data.
- Rosas, Mediano, Luppi, Boyd, Berta, Jensen, Gastpar & Carroll (2026), *Four faces of information in natural systems.* Nature Reviews Physics 8, 654–666.
  A current map of what information can mean in natural systems; useful background when choosing between decompositions.
- Dupret, Fusi & Panzeri (2026), *Neural population activity for memory: properties, computations, and codes.* Neuron 114, 390–407.
  Redundancy and synergy in memory codes. It does not equate redundancy with stability or synergy with flexibility; that mapping is our own extension.
- Antonacci, Barà, Sparacino, Marinazzo, Faes & Stramaglia (2026), *From statistical to structural synergy: a predictability framework to quantify the effects due to high-order mechanisms.* arXiv:2607.07286.
  Separates statistical synergy from synergy produced by genuine high-order mechanisms.
- Varley & Sporns (2022), *Multivariate information theory.* (tutorial/review).
  Review of multivariate IT measures.
- Robiglio, Neri, Iacopini, Petri & Barrat (2024), *Synergistic signatures of group mechanisms in higher-order systems.*
  Connects O-information to structural higher-order properties.
- Combrisson, Allegra, Basanisi, Ince, Giordano, Bastin, Petri & Brovelli (2025), *Higher-order and distributed synergistic functional interactions encode information gain in goal-directed learning.* Nature Communications.
  Synergy encodes information gain in learning.
- Kolchinsky (2022), *A novel approach to the partial information decomposition.* Entropy.
  Connects PID to the Information Bottleneck; clarifies axioms.
- Mediano, Rosas, Luppi, Carhart-Harris, Bor, Seth & Barrett (2021), *Towards an extended taxonomy of information dynamics via Integrated Information Decomposition.* arXiv:2109.13186
  Unifies integrated information and synergy under one framework (ΦID).
- Stramaglia, Faes, Cortes & Marinazzo (2024), *Disentangling high-order effects in the transfer entropy.* Physical Review Research 6:L032007
  Decomposes transfer entropy into unique/redundant/synergistic atoms (directed HOI).
- Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi (2014), *From the phenomenology to the mechanisms of consciousness: IIT 3.0.* PLoS Computational Biology.
  Integrated information theory, practical formalism.
- Albantakis et al. (2023), *Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0.* PLoS Computational Biology.
  Latest IIT formulation; intrinsic information.

## Renormalization on Networks

Coarse-graining networks and higher-order structures across scales while preserving what matters — dynamics, information, predictability. The across-agent analogue of the RG that organizes statistical mechanics.

- Villegas, Gili, Mulder, Caldarelli & Bianconi (2023), *Laplacian renormalization group for heterogeneous networks.* Nature Physics.
  Diffusion-based spectral coarse-graining. The key recent paper.
- García-Pérez, Boguñá & Serrano (2018), *Multiscale unfolding of real networks by geometric renormalization.* Nature Physics.
  Geometric (hidden-metric) approach to network RG.
- Serrano, Krioukov & Boguñá (2008), *Self-similarity of complex networks and hidden metric spaces.* Physical Review Letters 100, 078701
  Hidden hyperbolic geometry underpinning geometric RG.
- Koch-Janusz & Ringel (2018), *Mutual information, neural networks and the renormalization group.* Nature Physics.
  Information-theoretic / ML-driven RG.
- Nurisso, Morandini, Lucas, Vaccarino, Gili & Petri (2025), *Higher-order Laplacian renormalization.* Nature Physics.
  Cross-order Laplacians; order-specific scale invariance.
- Reitz & Bianconi (2020), *The higher-order spectrum of simplicial complexes: a renormalization group approach.* J. Phys. A.
  Higher-order spectral RG.
- Guadagnuolo, Nurisso, Galluzzi, Allard & Petri (2026), *Harmonic morphisms and dynamical invariants in network renormalization.* arXiv:2604.08386 (under review, PRX).
  Discrete harmonic morphisms as the minimal criterion for preserving random-walk dynamics under coarse-graining.
- Murphy, Thibeault, Allard & Desrosiers (2024), *Duality between predictability and reconstructability in complex systems.* Nature Communications.
  Coarse-graining trades reconstructability for predictability — a causal-emergence signature.
- Gabrielli, Garlaschelli, Patil & Serrano (2025), *Network renormalization.* Nature Reviews Physics 7, 203–219
  Comprehensive review of geometric, Laplacian and spectral RG on networks.
- Bradde & Bialek (2017), *PCA meets RG.* Journal of Statistical Physics.
  PCA and renormalization as the same idea.

## Criticality & Branching

Self-organized criticality, neuronal avalanches and branching processes as substrate-agnostic organizing principles across agents and scales — and the starting point for a perspective we are writing on branching across organs, fungi, cetaceans and AI.

- Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld (1987), *Self-organized criticality: an explanation of 1/f noise.* Physical Review Letters.
  Foundational SOC theory; avalanches and power laws.
- Beggs & Plenz (2003), *Neuronal avalanches in neocortical circuits.* Journal of Neuroscience.
  Empirical critical avalanche dynamics in neural tissue.
- Harris (1963), *The Theory of Branching Processes.* Springer.
  Classical theory: Galton–Watson processes, critical/super/subcritical regimes, extinction. Branching processes are the substrate for avalanches and criticality.
- Müller, Miron, Holtkamp & Meisel (2025), *Critical dynamics predicts cognitive performance and provides a common framework for heterogeneous mechanisms impacting cognition.* PNAS 122:e2417117122
  Proximity to criticality predicts cognition (intracranial EEG).
- Clauset, Shalizi & Newman (2009), *Power-law distributions in empirical data.* SIAM Review 51, 661–703.
  How to test a power law instead of eyeballing one: maximum-likelihood fits, goodness of fit, and comparison against heavy-tailed alternatives. The check every criticality claim has to pass.
- Lucas, Bisot, Petri, Declerck & Carletti (2026), *Minimal branching and fusion morphogenesis approaches biological multi-objective optimality.* arXiv:2601.03877
  Substrate-agnostic branching morphospace (fungal/vascular networks). A lab paper — Petri co-author.

## Topology Beyond One Parameter

Single-parameter persistence is the Tier 1 working tool. Real data rarely come with one natural filtration: density and scale, time and threshold, signal and noise pull in different directions. Multiparameter persistence is where topology has to go to handle that honestly, and its theory is still unsettled (no complete discrete invariant exists). We keep it here as the frontier our TDA work will meet, not as a method ready for routine use.

- Botnan & Lesnick (2023), *An introduction to multiparameter persistence.* arXiv:2203.14289
  NEW: frontier TDA — filtrations along multiple parameters.

## Operator Algebras & Quantum HOI

_Speculative: not part of the minimum._

**Speculative reading cluster — not part of the minimum.** Highly speculative direction spanning both branches: Fock-space structure of TPR, capacity limits as algebraic invariants, and a quantum analogue of higher-order synergy. HONEST STATUS: there is no mature "quantum HOI" literature yet — these are foundations for our exploratory work on quantum higher-order interactions, not an established canon.

- Smolensky (1990), *Tensor product variable binding.* (See Tier 1: Worldview.)
  The starting point: TPR and the 1/n collapse.
- Penrose (2004), *The Road to Reality.* (chapters on Hilbert spaces, operator algebras, Fock spaces).
  Accessible mathematical background for the speculation.
- Jansma (2023), *Higher-order interactions and their duals reveal synergy and logical dependence beyond Shannon information.* Entropy 25(4):648. arXiv:2205.04440
  Möbius-inversion view of synergy; a real bridge toward algebraic HOI.
- Upmeier (1987), *Jordan Algebras in Analysis, Operator Theory, and Quantum Mechanics.* CBMS Regional Conference Series in Mathematics 67, AMS
  Non-associative algebra in QM; scaffolding for a quantum-synergy analogue. (Speculative.)
