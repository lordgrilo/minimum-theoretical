# Criticality & Branching

Self-organized criticality, neuronal avalanches and branching processes as
substrate-agnostic organizing principles across systems and scales, from organs and
fungi to animal groups and artificial networks.

## Reading

- **Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld (1987)** — *Self-organized criticality: an explanation of 1/f noise.* Physical Review Letters. Foundational SOC theory; avalanches and power laws.
- **Beggs & Plenz (2003)** — *Neuronal avalanches in neocortical circuits.* Journal of Neuroscience. Empirical critical avalanche dynamics in neural tissue.
- **Harris (1963)** — *The Theory of Branching Processes.* Springer. Classical theory: Galton–Watson processes, critical/super/subcritical regimes, extinction. Branching processes are the substrate for avalanches and criticality.
- **Müller, Miron, Holtkamp & Meisel (2025)** — *Critical dynamics predicts cognitive performance and provides a common framework for heterogeneous mechanisms impacting cognition.* PNAS 122:e2417117122 Proximity to criticality predicts cognition (intracranial EEG).
- **Clauset, Shalizi & Newman (2009)** — *Power-law distributions in empirical data.* SIAM Review 51, 661–703. How to test a power law instead of eyeballing one: maximum-likelihood fits, goodness of fit, and comparison against heavy-tailed alternatives. The check every criticality claim has to pass.
- **Lucas, Bisot, Petri, Declerck & Carletti (2026)** — *Minimal branching and fusion morphogenesis approaches biological multi-objective optimality.* arXiv:2601.03877 Substrate-agnostic branching morphospace (fungal/vascular networks). A lab paper — Petri co-author.
- **West, Brown & Enquist (1997)** — *A general model for the origin of allometric scaling laws in biology.* Science 276, 122–126. Optimised space-filling transport networks yield quarter-power allometric scaling.
- **Katifori, Szöllősi & Magnasco (2010)** — *Damage and fluctuations induce loops in optimal transport networks.* Physical Review Letters 104, 048704. Damage and fluctuating load make loops optimal in transport networks.
- **Tero, Takagi, Saigusa, Ito, Bebber, Fricker, Yumiki, Kobayashi & Nakagaki (2010)** — *Rules for biologically inspired adaptive network design.* Science 327, 439–442. A slime mould builds transport networks comparable to engineered ones through local, flow-driven reinforcement.
- **Hannezo, Scheele, Moad, Drogo, Heer, Sampogna, van Rheenen & Simons (2017)** — *A unifying theory of branching morphogenesis.* Cell 171, 242–255. Branching organs grow by local stochastic rules, without a global plan.
