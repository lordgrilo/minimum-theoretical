# Semantic Horizons & Universal Laws

NPLab's most distinctive theoretical contribution and the flagship of the
within-agent arc. A system encoding stimuli with finite precision Δ and
compressing by β faces a *semantic horizon* r*(β) = −β log Δ beyond which
stimuli become indistinguishable — producing non-monotonic generalization and a
1/n capacity collapse that recovers Miller's Law from first principles. The
long-term bet (Keck) is that these are *universal* laws, the bridge from
within-agent limits to across-agent systems (immune repertoires, epidemics,
whale codas).

## Reading

- **Nurisso, Fernando, Deshpande, …, Cohen & Petri (2025)** — *Bound by Semanticity.* ICLR 2026. The central paper. Pareto frontier, 1/n collapse, Shepard's law derived. Read this first.
- **Savietto, Campbell, Panisson, Nurisso, Petri, Cohen & Perotti (2026)** — *The geometry of representational failures in vision-language models.* ICML 2026 (accepted). arXiv:2602.07025. Empirical companion — VLMs fail exactly where the framework predicts.
- **Petri, Musslick, Dey, Özcimder, Turner, Ahmed, Willke & Cohen (2021)** — *Topological limits to the parallel processing capability of network architectures.* Nature Physics. The precursor — the generalization–identification tradeoff in topological form.
- **Cover & Thomas Ch. 10 (rate-distortion) + Tishby et al. (2000) IB.** The mathematical engine: R(D) and the β-parameterized compression–relevance tradeoff with finite precision.
